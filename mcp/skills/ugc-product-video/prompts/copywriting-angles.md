# Copywriting angles

A good ad is several genuinely different bets on what will land, not one idea polished. After the brief ([brief-intake](brief-intake.md)), write two or three hook and script directions from different angles, then take one into [script-craft](script-craft.md).

1. **Reveal or story**: first person, present tense, "here's what just happened", the concrete sequence. The highest-context variant; let it run longer. ("Okay so I almost returned this. Two weeks in, my skin's calmer than it's been in a year.")
2. **Math or before and after**: quantify the old way against the new, with a real figure. ("Forty minutes on my routine. Now two products and four minutes. Same skin.")
3. **Fresh hook**: an angle nobody else runs, a pattern-breaking line that reframes the category. ("This isn't a skincare video. It's me apologizing to my old dermatologist.")
4. **Ultra-short CTA**: one claim, a couple of fast beats, one call to action; wins on cold traffic. ("Redness gone in two weeks. Link's in my bio.")
5. **Authority or community**: lead with a result, a count or a stat and what the viewer joins. ("Two thousand people switched last month. I finally get why.")

Swap-ins when they fit the offer better: **objection handler** (name the doubt, then dismantle it: "You're thinking it's another overhyped serum. So was I.") and **mechanism reveal** (show how it works or what's inside, not only the result).

Specificity beats adjectives: quantify ("47 redos gone", "14 days"), never "amazing results". When top-performing ads in the category are available, read why each works (hook pattern, proof point, CTA style) and carry that into the new variants without copying the words. No banned words ([craft](../references/craft.md)).

Offer the user two or three variants from different angles to choose between; generate the pick now and save the others in the storyboard overview so a second variant does not re-derive the brief.
